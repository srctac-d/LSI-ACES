# Transportable Molten Oxide Electrolysis (MOE) & Plasma Arc Crucible

## 1. Design Constraints & Transportable Sizing
To meet launch vehicle fairing and lander payload envelopes, the ISRU crucible assembly is scaled for compact transport and autonomous deployment:
* **Footprint:** Compact cylindrical envelope (1.5 m diameter x 2.2 m height) designed to fit standard medium-class lunar lander deck layouts[cite: 17].
* **Refractory & Shell Integration:** High-temperature ceramic lining encased in a lightweight structural alloy shell, pre-configured for immediate integration into the 10-foot staging workspace[cite: 17].

---

## 2. Core Heating & Oxidation Dynamics

### A. Dual-Stage Heating Architecture
* **Primary Plasma Arc:** Used for rapid initial melt phase, lowering raw regolith from ambient temperatures to a molten liquid slag phase (~1600°C)[cite: 17].
* **80kW IR Susceptor Array:** Surrounds the internal crucible volume to maintain steady-state bulk thermal equilibrium across the melt matrix[cite: 17].

### B. Secondary Power & Localized Spot Heat Control
* **Targeted Thermal Management:** Auxiliary localized secondary heating elements are positioned around critical high-viscosity zones, slag discharge ports, and tapping channels[cite: 17].
* **Freeze-Up Prevention:** Independent spot power modulation prevents freeze-ups at the tapping point during stratified liquid metal extraction without requiring full-crucible thermal spikes[cite: 17].

### C. Internal Electrodes & Oxygen Harvesting
* **Submerged Oxygen-Evolving Anode (Inside Electrode):** Positioned directly inside the molten regolith/slag matrix[cite: 17].
* **Direct Reduction:** Drives electrochemical decomposition ($2\text{O}^{2-} \rightarrow \text{O}_2 + 4e^-$), releasing high-purity gaseous oxygen ($\text{O}_2$) into upper collector hoods while reducing metal oxides ($\text{Fe}$, $\text{Si}$, $\text{Al}$) into a dense metallic phase at the bottom cathode[cite: 17].

---

## 3. Discharge & Stratified Extraction
* **Single-Tap Stratified Sump:** Utilizes density separation of the molten bath[cite: 17]. Heavy iron and silicon alloys settle into the bottom sump for controlled tapping, while lighter slag remains in the upper processing zone[cite: 17].
* **Direct Feed to Downstream Processes:** Tapped molten metals feed directly into net-shape casting molds or fiber-drawing units, minimizing re-heating energy overhead[cite: 17].

---

## 4. Sequence of Operations & Automation Logic

1. **Pre-Heating & Atmospheric Purge:** The sealed crucible is evacuated to vacuum and backfilled with inert carrier gas ($\text{Ar}$/$\text{N}_2$).
2. **Batch Regolith Insertion:** Planetary screw augers feed raw un-sintered lunar regolith into the upper crucible charge hopper.
3. **Primary Melt Activation:** The non-transferred plasma arc strikes the regolith bed, establishing a liquid molten pool within 45 minutes.
4. **Electrolysis & Oxygen Collection:** The submerged iridium/ruthenium-coated anode energizes, initiating steady-state oxygen gas generation routed to cryo-liquefaction storage tanks.
5. **Stratified Tap Release:** Secondary induction spot heating energizes the bottom discharge nozzle, tapping molten iron/silicon into structural mold beds.

---

## 5. In-Situ Crystalline Optics & Thermal Processing (Volume 33)

### 5.1 Dual-Beam Solar Arc Calcination & Concentrated Optics
* **Primary Inputs:** Unrefined regolith feed and focused solar flux delivered via direct or optical fiber collector systems[cite: 16, 17].
* **Process Dynamics:** Dual-beam solar arc concentration drives rapid thermal calcination, stripping atmospheric volatiles prior to liquid melt phase.

### 5.2 Net-Shape Crystalline Synthesis & Synthetic Sapphire Growth
* **Material Output:** Optical-grade synthetic sapphire, fused aluminosilicate matrices, and net-shape structural transparent elements.
* **Refinery Feed Pipeline:** Direct thermal coupling with MOE slag output allows low-energy conversion of refined alumina ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$) and silica ($\text{SiO}_2$) into high-purity crystalline windows, lenses, and optical waveguides[cite: 17].
